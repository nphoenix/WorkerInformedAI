# Worker-Informed Participation Protocol (WIPP)

**Version 1.0**  
**Date:** June 2026  
**Repository:** [WorkerInformedAI](https://github.com/nphoenix/WorkerInformedAI)

## Purpose and Scope

The **Worker-Informed Participation Protocol (WIPP)** establishes a mandatory framework for meaningful worker involvement in the design, development, procurement, modification, and deployment of any software, AI, algorithmic, or automated system (collectively, "System") that may impact worker health, safety, psychological well-being, job quality, performance evaluation, scheduling, monitoring, or workload.

WIPP shifts workers from passive subjects to **active, informed co-participants**. It prioritizes non-maleficence ("do no harm"), privacy-by-design, and human-centered outcomes.

**Scope**: Applies to all new or materially modified Systems with potential health/safety implications, including AI-driven monitoring, scheduling tools, performance analytics, exposure assessment systems, and decision-support platforms.

## Core Principles

- **Informed Participation**: Workers and their representatives receive timely, accessible, and comprehensible information about system purpose, data practices, risks, logic, and impacts.
- **Meaningful Voice**: Participation includes genuine opportunities for input, co-design, feedback, and substantive influence on decisions.
- **Equity and Inclusion**: Mechanisms must accommodate diverse roles, shifts, languages, abilities, and protected characteristics. No retaliation for participation or raising concerns.
- **Transparency and Accountability**: All documentation, assessments, and decisions are auditable and shared with worker representatives.
- **Integration with Standards**: Builds upon NIST AI Risk Management Framework (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage), participatory design methodologies, UC Berkeley Labor Center *Negotiating Tech* inventory, and OWASP AI Security & Privacy Guide.

## Mandatory Requirements

For any new or materially modified System:

### a. Early-Stage Involvement
Worker representatives (selected through union or democratic workplace processes) shall participate in needs assessment, problem definition, and requirements gathering. This includes joint workshops using participatory design methods (co-ideation, scenario mapping, prototype feedback).

### b. Collaborative Impact Assessment
A joint Worker-Employer Impact Assessment Team shall evaluate:
- Physical and psychological health & safety risks
- Privacy, data rights, and surveillance effects
- Bias, fairness, and disparate impacts
- Workload, autonomy, and job quality implications

Assessments must be completed, documented, and disclosed **before** design or procurement proceeds.

### c. Iterative Co-Design and Testing
Workers shall participate in iterative design reviews, usability testing, simulation pilots, and safety validation. All worker feedback must be documented with clear rationale for acceptance or rejection.

### d. Pre-Deployment Approval Gate
High-risk features require explicit review and approval by the joint team (or designated worker representatives) prior to rollout. Deployment may be paused or conditioned on mitigations.

### e. Ongoing Governance
Establish a standing **Joint Worker Participation Committee** with balanced or worker-majority representation for continuous monitoring, annual reviews, incident response, and system updates. Workers shall have access to system outputs and appropriate override mechanisms for high-stakes decisions.

### f. Training and Capacity Building
Employers shall provide paid, protected time for training on the System, risk evaluation, and participatory processes.

## Documentation and Compliance

- Maintain a living **WIPP Record** (version-controlled repository) documenting all participation activities, decisions, rationales, and assessment results.
- Align with external tools: NIST AI RMF Playbook, OWASP AI guides, and Berkeley Labor Center contract examples.
- Violations or unresolved safety concerns trigger grievance procedures, independent audits, or deployment halts.

## Adaptability and Review

This clause is designed to be adaptable by mutual agreement while preserving core participation rights. It shall be reviewed annually or upon significant technological change, with worker input required.
